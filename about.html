About Mangaldeep

How a school for children with special needs began in the hills of Almora.

Many years ago

Jagdish Chandra Joshi, then Principal of an Almora college and well recognised in the academic world, found his life changed after working closely with an autistic child. He saw at close quarters how helpless a child with a disability could be in the beautiful but harsh terrain of the Himalaya.

In cities, children with disabilities have awareness, institutions, funds, skilled teachers and buses to special schools. In the mountains it is a different world: small farms with sparse crops, poor households, alcoholism, and parents struggling to earn a living while bringing up their children. Children walk 4 to 10 km a day to reach school, often without proper warm clothes in winter.

A poor family has neither the time, the money nor the knowledge to care for a child who is mentally challenged, deaf and mute, blind, or living with several disabilities. Such children were often pitied, hidden away, and sometimes beaten, tied up or shut in a room by despairing families. Neglected, they became withdrawn, and their lives were wasted.

Moved by these unheard voices, Shri Jagdish Joshi and his sister Ms Manorama Joshi set up Mangaldeep as a registered society in 1998. Another family member, the late Shri RS Joshi, helped in the early years by paying teacher salaries and providing his vehicle and driver to bring the children to school.

Two young teachers, Ruchi Purohit and Vinita Bhatt, were trained and joined at the start. Their devotion to the children, and their readiness to keep learning new methods, shaped the school’s culture of compassion, care and professionalism.

Mangaldeep began with six children and two teachers. Today it has nearly 50 students with different disabilities and a staff of 12. Shri Jagdish Joshi stepped down in 2013 for health reasons, and Sushri Manorama Joshi now heads the institution.
